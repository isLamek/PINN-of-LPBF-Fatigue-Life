Phase 2: where cracks start

A peridynamic model of each measured section finds where the crack starts: at the largest defect in 8 of 14 sections, not the 90% expected.

Governing equation

Bond-based peridynamics: each point $x$ is held in equilibrium by bonds to every point within its horizon $\delta$, $\int_{H_x} c\,s\,\chi\,\frac{\xi+\eta}{|\xi+\eta|}\,dV_{x'} + b(x) = 0$, with bond stretch $s=(|\xi+\eta|-|\xi|)/|\xi|$. Under cyclic load each bond loses life as $d\lambda/dN = -A(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{th})^{\beta}$ and breaks ($\chi=0$) when $\lambda$ reaches zero.

Numerical scheme

The measured section through the governing defect on a uniform grid, Δx = 40 µm, horizon δ = 3Δx, with defects as voids, in plane stress. Equilibrium is solved as a sparse linear system at each damage event; time jumps to the next bond failure.

Verification

As the horizon is refined the model's elastic modulus converges to the classical value: error 13.8% at m = 2 falling to 0.29% at m = 8 (chart below).

Result

The first bond breaks at the largest defect in 8 of 14 measured sections (57.1%); H2 asked for 90%.

This phase has two jobs. It measures every defect, and the size of the largest goes to Phase 3 to become the life. Its crack model then shows where the crack starts and which way it runs. The crack model's cycle count is not used as a life: at attainable grid resolution it cannot carry one.

From a defect to a number

Every defect is isolated in three dimensions and reduced to the size fracture mechanics uses: the square root of its area projected on the plane across the load, √area (Murakami). The catalogue holds 1,342 defects from 14 cylinders, the largest 1,049 µm. Flatness separates lack-of-fusion flaws (about 0.1) from round gas pores (about 1).

Verification: the model converges to classical elasticity

Refining the horizon drives the error from 13.8% to 0.29% (fitted order 2.31). The decline is not smooth because the number of bonds per node changes in steps. The bond-based form fixes Poisson's ratio at 1/3 in plane stress, against about 0.30 for 316H; that is a property of the formulation, which is why a finite element model runs alongside as a cross-check.

Hypothesis 2: does the crack start at the largest defect?

Criterion not met: 8 of 14 (57%) against 90%. By Murakami severity, 9 of 14.

Every bond starts with the same life and loses it fastest where it is stretched most, so the first bond to break is the most strained bond of the initial elastic solution: one solve per section, independent of the damage coefficient and of how many bonds the model breaks per step. Size alone does not decide where a crack starts. 4 of 14 sections started at a surface defect, which Murakami's geometry factors make 1.69 times as severe per unit area as a buried one; weighting for that lifts the count only to 9 of 14, so neighbouring defects matter too. This concerns the severity criterion, not the segmentation or the life model, which uses defect size and is tested in Phase 4.
