Phase 3: the fatigue life

A crack grown from the largest defect predicts the life to within 1.36× with nothing fitted, and its scatter reproduces the lives of 46 further cylinders.

Governing equation

The driving force on a crack of depth $a$ is $\Delta K = Y\,\Delta\sigma\sqrt{\pi a}$ and the Paris law gives its growth rate, $da/dN = C(\Delta K-\Delta K_{th})^{m}$. The life is the number of cycles to grow it from the governing defect, $a_0=\sqrt{\text{area}}$, to the depth $a_f$ at which the section collapses: $N=\int_{a_0}^{a_f} da\,/\,C(\Delta K(a)-\Delta K_{th})^{m}$.

Numerical scheme

Composite Simpson's rule on a logarithmically spaced grid in $a$, since the integrand is concentrated at the lower limit. Scatter is carried by Monte Carlo over the defect size and the growth rate; inspections update one part's life through a particle filter.

Verification

Against the closed form the error falls at order 4.0 to 4.4, as Simpson's rule should, reaching 6e-14 at 801 points before round-off (table below).

Result

Within 1.36× of the measured lives of the 14 held-out cylinders, with no constant fitted to any fatigue life.

The constants are literature values for 316L: C = 6.25 × 10⁻¹⁰ and m = 3.94 (da/dN in mm per cycle, ΔK in MPa√m), and Y = 0.65, Murakami's factor for a surface defect. None of them is fitted to this dataset's fatigue lives.

The one modelling decision: the threshold

The long-crack threshold for 316L is ΔK_th = 5 MPa√m. At the governing gauge defect of the held-out cylinders the driving force lies between 4.4 and 14.9 MPa√m, close to that threshold or below it. Kept in, it predicts that 1 cylinder never fails and over-predicts the other 13 by a median factor of 12.7, although every one of them failed in test. Cracks from defects this small are short cracks, to which the long-crack threshold does not apply, so the validated prediction, path A, sets ΔK_th = 0.

Monte Carlo: a distribution of lives

Parts printed the same way differ. Each virtual part draws a governing defect from a lognormal fitted to the 14 measured sizes, $\ln a_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(6.013, 0.433^2)$ with $a_0$ in µm, and a growth rate $C$ from the scatter of path A's own errors: the $C$ that would have made each of the 14 predictions exact, $\sigma(\ln C) = 0.308$. It then goes through path A's life integral, threshold removed, so the uncertainty carried is that of the validated prediction.

The distribution building up as draws arrive, 20,000 draws in all. Median life 431,166 cycles; run-outs 0.0%.

Against the measured record. Both priors come from the 14 held-out cylinders, so the other 23 tested at 300 MPa are independent of the simulation. Their median life is 420,016 cycles against 431,166 simulated; 17 of their 19 failures fall inside the simulated 5th to 95th percentile band, and a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test finds no difference (p = 0.69).

Where it falls short. 4 of those cylinders ran out at 10 million cycles without failing; the simulation predicts no run-outs, because with the threshold removed every crack grows. A threshold that grows with crack length would be needed to reproduce the fatigue limit.

The distribution building up as draws arrive, 20,000 draws in all. Median life 82,019 cycles; run-outs 0.0%.

Against the measured record. Both priors come from the 14 held-out cylinders, so the other 23 tested at 450 MPa are independent of the simulation. Their median life is 81,400 cycles against 82,019 simulated; 19 of their 23 failures fall inside the simulated 5th to 95th percentile band, and a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test finds no difference (p = 0.33).

Convergence. The Dvoretzky–Kiefer–Wolfowitz inequality bounds how far the sampled distribution can lie from the true one: $P\big(\sup_N |\hat F_M(N)-F(N)| > \varepsilon\big) \le 2e^{-2M\varepsilon^2}$. For ε = 0.05 at 95% confidence it needs M ≥ 738 draws. The run used M = 20,000, which gives ε = 0.0096.

Particle filter: one part's life as inspections arrive

Once a part is in service, each crack-length reading narrows its life. 4,000 particles, each a possible growth rate $C_i$, are reweighted by how well they predict the reading and then resampled. Crack size and rate separate in the Paris integral, so each particle's history is one shared function:

$$S(a) = \int_{a_0}^{a} \frac{da^\prime}{\big(\Delta K(a^\prime)-\Delta K_{th}\big)^{m}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad a_i(N) = S^{-1}(C_i\,N)$$

Hypothesis 3 met on the recovery trial: within 20% of the true life at inspection 1, against 3 hypothesised; effective sample size never below 1,205, against 250.

That trial's true growth rate fell near the centre of the prior, where the prior alone is already within 20%. Placed at the prior's 5th and 95th percentiles instead, where the prior's life is off by 40% to 66%, the filter still came within 20% by inspection 3 in all 20 trials. The effective sample size fell below 250 in 2 of them, to 148, before resampling restored it: the speed condition holds throughout, the sample-size condition fails at the tails.
