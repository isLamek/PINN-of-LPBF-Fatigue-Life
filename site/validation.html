Phase 4: validation

With nothing fitted, path A (1.36×) beats every fitted model, including a 113-parameter physics-informed neural network (1.55×).

Governing equation

Theory fixes the form of a fatigue model. Neglecting the threshold, the life integral is $N_f = a_0^{1-m/2}/\big[(m/2-1)\,C\,(Y\Delta\sigma\sqrt{\pi})^{m}\big]$, so $\log_{10} N_f = b_0 + b_s\ln\Delta\sigma + b_a\ln\sqrt{\text{area}}$ with $b_s=-m/\ln 10$ and $b_a=(1-m/2)/\ln 10$, both negative.

Numerical scheme

A ladder of regressions from stress alone up to four parameters, the same regression with the signs of $b_s$ and $b_a$ bound (B3), a physics-informed neural network (C1) whose loss penalises its own gradients for leaving the theoretical slopes, and a correction factor φ with the physical signs built into its weights. Every model is scored leave-one-out on the 14 cylinders with a measured defect.

Verification

Fitted to lives synthesised with known exponents, the regression recovers them with error falling at order 1.00 as the scatter falls, the rate of an unbiased linear estimator.

Result

Path A, with nothing fitted, is within 1.36×. The best fit needs 4 parameters for 1.43×; the network 113 for 1.55×. Hypothesis 4 on B3: 13 of 14 inside its interval, slope 0.902.

Leave-one-out: to score a model on one cylinder, fit it to the other thirteen, predict the one left out, and repeat for every cylinder. No prediction is made by a model that has seen its answer.

The data

60 cylinders of LPBF 316H were fatigue tested (ASTM E466, R = 0.1, at 300 and 450 MPa): 56 failed and 4 ran out at 10 million cycles. Of the failures, 41 broke in the gauge and 15 at the fillet radius. 14 have a governing defect measured from their CT scan; they carry every model below that uses defect size.

Murakami's rule treats two defects closer than the smaller one's √area as a single defect. Applied here it makes every affected prediction worse. For P017 the merged flaw exceeds the depth at which the section collapses, a life of zero, while the single governing defect predicts its measured life almost exactly. The 400 µm slice spacing biases the rule toward merging, but the data favour the single defect.

The correction factor φ

φ multiplies a baseline life, N = N_base / φ. By the brief's physical intuition it must rise with defect size and fall with circularity and with distance from the surface. The network carries those signs in its weights: every weight between an input and φ passes through a softplus and is positive, and circularity and ligament enter with a minus sign. A model that breaks a sign rule cannot be represented.

$$N = \frac{N_{\text{base}}}{\varphi(\ln\sqrt{\text{area}},\,-\Psi,\,-L)},\qquad \frac{\partial \ln\varphi}{\partial z_k}\ \ge 0$$

Verification. On synthetic lives built with a known φ, the trained network's error in log₁₀ φ falls from 0.075 at n = 14, 0.069 at n = 56, 0.046 at n = 224. Its weight decay (0.001) was fixed by that study before any real fit. The sign rules hold at 2,000 random test points.

Reported as found. On the 14 cylinders, neither φ improves on path A. Fitted to all 14, the sign-bounded linear φ sets every coefficient to zero: once the physical signs are enforced, the data ask for no correction for size, shape or location beyond what the crack-growth law already contains. With 14 specimens this cannot rule such effects out; it shows they are below what this dataset can resolve.

What ends the part

At the collapse depth the stress intensity reaches at most 23.0 MPa√m, against a fracture toughness of about 200 MPa√m, a margin of 9. 316 steel is too tough to fracture fast in a 5 mm bar: the part fails when the cracked section can no longer carry the load, which is how path A defines the final crack depth.

Limits

  • Only 14 of the 60 cylinders have a measured defect size; the ladder's A rungs use bulk porosity, an average where the physics asks for the extreme.
  • With 14 points the leave-one-out scores carry real variance; read the ladder as indicative, not as a certified ranking.
  • Path A's threshold of zero is a short-crack argument, supported by the threshold sweep in Phase 3, not a measured short-crack law.
