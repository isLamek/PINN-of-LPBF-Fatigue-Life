Conclusions

Three of the four hypotheses are met. The one that is not, where cracks start, returns a clear negative that points at the severity criterion.

Each objective was tied to a hypothesis with an acceptance criterion fixed before testing. Each outcome below is read from the result files.

H1. Segmentation accuracy

Accepted only if the mean Dice on withheld data is at least 0.80.

Criterion met: Dice 0.805.

Mean over five folds, 0.794 to 0.825 per fold. The network finds the defects well enough for their size to be trusted.

H2. Crack initiation site

Accepted only if the crack starts at the largest defect in at least 90% of sections.

Criterion not met: 8 of 14 (57.1%).

Ranked by Murakami severity instead of size, 9 of 14. Size alone does not decide where a crack starts: closeness to the surface and neighbouring defects matter too.

H3. Bayesian convergence

Accepted only if the posterior is within 20% of the true life by the third inspection with an effective sample size of at least 250.

Criterion met: within 20% at inspection 1; effective sample size at least 1,205.

With the true growth rate placed in the tails of the prior instead, the filter still converges by the third inspection, but its effective sample size can dip below 250 before resampling restores it. The speed condition holds throughout; the sample-size condition fails where the prior is badly wrong.

H4. Predictive calibration

Accepted only if at least 90% of measured lives fall inside the 90% interval and the calibration slope lies between 0.90 and 1.10.

Criterion met: 13 of 14 inside (92.9%); slope 0.902.

Tested on B3, with the interval built from its leave-one-out residual spread. The slope passes at the lower edge.

What this work shows

  • A part's own CT scan predicts its own fatigue life to within 1.36× on held-out cylinders, with nothing fitted to the fatigue lives.
  • The best statistical fit needs 4 parameters to reach 1.43×; a physics-informed neural network with 113 reaches 1.55×. Measuring the defect does the work that fitting cannot.
  • Propagated through the same model, the scatter in defect size and growth rate reproduces the measured life distribution of 46 further cylinders not used to build it.
  • Each numerical method was checked against a known answer before it was used on measured data, and a life can be followed by hand from defect size to cycles.

A part with a benign defect population can be kept in service instead of scrapped on a batch rule, and a part with a severe one withdrawn before it fails, with the uncertainty stated rather than hidden in a safety factor.

Limits

  • One alloy and one dataset: 316H from one machine and one powder lot, with fourteen held-out cylinders carrying the validation.
  • Where a crack starts is not predicted by size alone (H2).
  • The peridynamic model cannot carry an absolute life at attainable grid resolution; it gives the site and the path, and the life comes from the crack-growth integral.
  • Fillet-radius failures start at a stress concentration, not a defect, and lie outside the defect-driven model.
  • With the threshold removed for short cracks, the model predicts no run-outs, while 4 of the 23 independent cylinders at 300 MPa ran out.

What comes next

  • A severity measure with surface and interaction terms, to re-test H2.
  • A threshold that grows from the short-crack to the long-crack value as the crack lengthens, so the population model reproduces the run-outs at 300 MPa.
  • A stress-concentration model at the fillet radius, so both failure locations are covered.
  • Each specimen's own Bayesian interval for H4, in place of the residual-based one.
